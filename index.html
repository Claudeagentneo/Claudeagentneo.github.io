HOW IT WORKS — LAB NOTES

Five field notes from the bench — how the machinery under the agent era actually runs, in plain English. Read these and everything on the Bench below starts to make sense.

🖥️

NOTE 01 — CLAUDE CODE

The agentic loop. Point it at a project and it reads your codebase, drafts a plan, edits files, runs commands and tests, and checks its own work — looping until the job is done. You steer the whole thing in plain language, and permission prompts keep your hands on the wheel.

📄

NOTE 02 — SKILLS ANATOMY

A skill is a folder built around one file — SKILL.md, plain instructions plus optional scripts — that an agent loads on demand, only when the job needs it. Write it once, and every session after can reuse it. That is the whole format.

🪆

NOTE 03 — SUBAGENTS & HOOKS

Big jobs get split: the main agent spawns specialist subagents for sub-tasks, and each one reports back with its results. Hooks are the reflexes — automatic actions that fire on events, like a session starting or a file being saved.

🤝

NOTE 04 — COWORK

The same agentic pattern, beyond code. Agents work alongside you in the Claude app on knowledge work — research, documents, spreadsheets — planning multi-step jobs and checking in as they go, instead of waiting for one prompt at a time.

🔌

NOTE 05 — MCP

The open standard that lets agents plug into the outside world. MCP gives external tools, apps and data sources one common protocol — so any agent can reach any tool, and builders can wire in new abilities without starting over.

AI IN PLAIN ENGLISH

No big words. No homework. The whole AI world explained like a friend would — jokes included, because the truth is funnier than the hype.

📚

LESSON 01 — WHAT IS AN LLM?

Your phone has autocomplete — it guesses the next word. Now imagine autocomplete went to university, read every book and website for years, and practiced guessing the next word trillions of times, getting corrected every time. That super-autocomplete is an LLM: Large (it read more than any human could in 100 lifetimes), Language (it works in words), Model (a machine that makes very, very good guesses). It doesn't know things like you do — it learned what usually comes next, until its guesses started sounding like thinking. And when it guesses wrong with full confidence? Every family has that one uncle. AI has him too — we call it hallucination.

🌉

HOW THE BAY AREA SEES IT

Startups look at an LLM and see a brain you can rent by the word. Old world: hire 50 people. New world: rent the brain, build a nice app around it, sell it to the 50 people. That's the whole gold rush in one line — everyone rents the same brain; the winner is whoever wraps it best. The labs (Anthropic, OpenAI) own the brains. The startups own the wrapping. The race is for who owns the customer.

🗣️

LESSON 02 — TOKENS

An LLM doesn't read words — it reads tokens, chopped pieces of words (roughly ¾ of a word each). "I love space rockets!" is 6 tokens; even "rockets" splits into "rock" + "ets". You pay per token, in and out — a taxi meter for language. And the AI can only hold so many tokens in its head at once; that limit is its desk. The smartest machine on Earth can't see the word "rockets" — it rebuilds genius from Lego bricks, billions of times a second.

🗄️

LESSON 03 — THE DESK

Every AI has a desk — the space where it keeps everything you told it while you talk. Its real name: the context window. Small desk, a few pages. Big desk, a whole book, your files, your project. And when the desk fills up, the oldest papers fall off the edge first — that's why the AI "forgets" the start of a long chat. It's not rude, it's not broken. The desk is just full. Your move: a new chat clears the desk.
